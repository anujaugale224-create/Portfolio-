Digital FabLab · Module 3
Computer-Aided Design (CAD)
📐

Module Overview

A 7-day journey into Computer-Aided Design — starting with basic 2D sketching commands, moving through 3D extrusion and assembly design, modeling standard mechanical parts like nuts and bolts, designing gears and understanding mechanical motion, and finishing with a complete AutoCAD button design project. Click on any day to view notes, topics covered, and screenshots.

Day 1
Sketching Basics
Line, Rectangle, Circle & their sub-types
▼

Day 1 introduced the basics of sketching in a CAD environment — the foundation every 3D model is built on. We learned the core drawing commands and a few essential selection and editing shortcuts.

  • Learned the basics of how to sketch, and the core drawing commands: Line, Rectangle, and Circle, along with their sub-types.
  • Selecting a closed loop: double-clicking selects the entire chain of connected lines, provided the diagram (loop) is closed.
  • Creating a construction line: select the line and press X to toggle it to a construction line.
Steps Followed
  • Draw a sketch, starting from the top view.
📝

Practice: Sketch a simple shape from the top view using the Line, Rectangle, and Circle tools, then practice selecting a closed loop with a double-click and converting a line to a construction line using the X shortcut.

Day 2
Sketch Selection & Construction Lines
Selecting closed loops, construction geometry
▼

Day 2 built on the sketching basics — practicing the same core commands (Line, Rectangle, Circle and their sub-types) while going deeper into selection techniques and construction geometry.

  • Practiced sketching using Line, Rectangle, Circle, and their sub-types.
  • Double-clicking selects the whole chain of connected lines when the diagram is closed.
  • To make a construction line: select the line and press X.
Output
📝

Practice: Create a closed sketch, select the full loop using a double-click, and convert at least one line into a construction line.

Day 3
3D Extrude & Design Approaches
Part, Assembly & Hybrid Design, navigation shortcuts
▼

Day 3 moved from 2D sketches into 3D — using the Extrude command to turn flat profiles into solid bodies, and learning the different design approaches used in CAD software.

  • Draw a sketch, then use Extrude to begin 3D designing.
  • Three 3D design approaches: Part Design, Assembly Design, and Hybrid Design.
  • An extruded profile (Part) can be combined using Body, Join, or Cut operations.
  • Individual Parts are combined together to form an Assembly.
  • Pan shortcut: click and hold the mouse scroll (middle) button.
  • Orbit shortcut: mouse scroll (middle) button + Shift.
Output
📝

Practice: Sketch a 2D profile, extrude it into a solid body, and practice navigating the 3D workspace using the Pan and Orbit shortcuts.

Day 4
Mechanical Parts — Nut & Bolt
Modeling standard fasteners and their differences
▼

Day 4 introduced basic mechanical parts by modeling two of the most common fasteners — a nut and a bolt — and discussing how they differ in form and function.

  • Learned about basic mechanical parts, focusing on the nut and bolt.
  • Discussed the difference between a nut and a bolt.
  • Modeled a bolt as a hex head with a threaded shaft, and a nut as a hex body with internal threads, as separate components within one assembly.
Output
📝

Practice: Model a hex-head bolt with a threaded shaft and a matching hex nut with internal threads, then position them together as an assembly.

Day 5
Gear Design and Rotation
Gear teeth, motion & interaction between gears
▼

Day 5 covered gear design — one of the most fundamental mechanical components used to transmit motion and power.

  • Learned to design gears in a CAD environment.
  • Practiced creating and arranging gear teeth.
  • Used rotation tools to understand the movement and interaction between gears.
  • Built an understanding of basic mechanical motion and how CAD is used to design mechanical components.
📝

Practice: Design a gear with evenly arranged teeth, then use rotation tools to simulate how it meshes and interacts with a second gear.

Day 6
Button Design in AutoCAD
2D drawing, editing tools & precise dimensioning
▼

Day 6 focused on precision drafting — designing a button entirely in AutoCAD using fundamental 2D drawing and editing tools.

  • Designed a button in AutoCAD using basic 2D drawing and editing tools.
  • Practiced creating geometric shapes and applying accurate dimensions.
  • Modified the design to achieve the required shape.
  • Improved understanding of CAD drafting, precision, and digital product design.
📝

Practice: Draft a button design in AutoCAD using 2D geometric shapes, applying precise dimensions, and refining the shape through editing tools.

Day 7
Appearance & Construction
Finishing the button project, new tool sections
▼

Day 7 wrapped up the button project from Day 6 and introduced two new areas of the CAD workspace: Appearance and Construction.

  • Completed the button design project.
  • Learned the Appearance section, used to apply materials and finishes to a model.
  • Learned a new section: Construction, used for construction geometry tools.
Output
📝

Practice: Finish the button project, then apply an appearance/material to the finished model and explore the Construction tools.
